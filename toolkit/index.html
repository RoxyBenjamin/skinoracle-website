Most skin-care ingredients are fine together; a few strong actives are better at different times of day or on different nights, because together they irritate more and each does less. The usual splits: vitamin C in the morning and retinoids at night; retinoids and exfoliating acids on different nights; sunscreen every morning with any of them.

Can I use retinol and vitamin C together?

Yes, just not at the same time. They work best at different times of day: vitamin C in the morning, under your sunscreen, and retinol in the evening. Used together they can be harsher on skin and each works less well.

Can I use niacinamide with vitamin C?

For most people they're fine together. If your skin feels irritated or flushed, split them: vitamin C in the morning and niacinamide at night, or alternate mornings.

Can I use AHA and BHA together?

Usually best on different days. Glycolic and salicylic acid both exfoliate, and together they can over-exfoliate, leaving skin dry and irritated. Alternate them, or use one regularly and the other only now and then.

Cost per use is the price divided by the number of uses in the pack. A $48, 30 ml serum used a few drops at a time holds about 100 uses, so each use costs about 48 cents, and a product you skip costs more per use than one you love and use every night.

How do I work out the cost per use of a skin-care product?

Divide the size by the amount you use each time to get the number of uses, then divide the price by that number. The tool does it for you.

Is a more expensive product better value?

Not by itself. What’s inside, how much you need and how often you really use it decide the value, not the price on the box.

Ingredient lists run from the most to the least, down to about 1%; after that they can be in any order. So the first five or so ingredients make up most of the product, and the actives you paid for are worth finding in that list.

How do I read a skin-care ingredient list?

Start at the top: the first few ingredients are most of the formula. Water (aqua) is usually first. Look for the active you wanted and see roughly where it sits.

Is fragrance bad for skin?

Not for everyone, but scent is one of the most common reasons sensitive skin reacts. If your skin is easily upset, fragrance-free is a good place to start.

The little open-jar symbol on a pack, with a number like 12M, is the “period after opening”: how many months a product stays at its best once it’s opened. Count from the day you opened it, and replace it sooner if the smell, colour or texture changes.

What does the open-jar symbol on skin care mean?

It’s the period after opening: the number of months a product is at its best once opened, like 12M for 12 months.

How do I know if skin care has gone off?

A changed smell, colour or texture is the sign. Replace it then, whatever the date says.

A good starter routine is short: cleanser, moisturizer and sunscreen in the morning; cleanser and moisturizer at night. Add one active at a time, matched to what matters most to you, once the basics are a habit.

What is the simplest good skin-care routine?

Morning: cleanser, moisturizer, sunscreen. Evening: cleanser and moisturizer. Add one active at a time once that is a habit.

How many skin-care products do I need?

Fewer than most people think. Three to five, used every day, beats a full shelf used now and then.

Myth or fact? Tap to find out

Many skin-care “rules” are marketing, not science. Oily skin still needs moisturizer, tingling usually means irritation rather than results, and “natural” doesn’t always mean gentle.

Do I need sunscreen on cloudy days?

Yes. Much of the sun’s UV gets through clouds.

Does tingling mean a skin-care product is working?

No. Tingling often means irritation, not results.
