THE BARRIER

What is the skin barrier, and why does it matter?

Your skin barrier is the outermost layer of your skin: flat cells held together by fats, a bit like bricks and mortar. It keeps water in and irritants out, and when it’s worn down, skin tends to feel dry, tight and quick to sting.

Read the answer → ACTIVES

What does “active ingredient” mean in skin care?

An “active” is an ingredient added to change how skin looks or behaves over time, like a retinoid, an exfoliating acid or vitamin C. Actives can do a lot, which is exactly why more isn’t better: the right one, used steadily, beats several used at once.

Read the answer → LAYERING

In what order should I apply skin care?

Apply skin care from the thinnest texture to the thickest, after cleansing, and finish with sunscreen in the morning. Some actives work better at different times of day, so both the order and the pairing matter.

Read the answer → THE SUN

Do I really need sunscreen every day?

Yes: daily sunscreen is the most protective step in any routine, because UV reaches skin on cloudy days and through windows, and its effects add up over years. Use enough (about two finger-lengths for face and neck), choose broad spectrum SPF 30 or higher, and reapply every two hours outdoors.
