The science · the barrier

What is the skin barrier, and why does it matter?

Your skin barrier is the outermost layer of your skin: flat cells held together by fats, a bit like bricks and mortar. It keeps water in and irritants out, and when it’s worn down, skin tends to feel dry, tight and quick to sting.

What it’s made of

  • Skin cells are the bricks; fats such as ceramides, cholesterol and fatty acids are the mortar between them.

What wears it down

  • Washing too often, or with hot water.
  • Too many strong actives at once, or starting them too fast.
  • Very dry air, cold wind and long hot showers.
  • Harsh cleansers and, for some skin, fragrance.

How to look after it

  • A gentle cleanser, once or twice a day.
  • A moisturizer with barrier fats (ceramides, cholesterol, fatty acids).
  • Fewer actives, started slowly, one at a time.
  • Sunscreen every morning.

When to see a doctor

  • If skin stays sore, cracked or bleeding, or doesn’t settle after a couple of weeks of gentle care, please see your doctor.

Questions people ask

How do I know if my skin barrier is damaged?

Skin that suddenly stings with products it used to like, feels tight and dry all day, or looks red and flaky is often a sign the barrier needs a rest. Go back to a gentle cleanser, moisturizer and sunscreen for a while.
